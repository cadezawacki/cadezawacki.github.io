03 / THE IDEA

Borrow the hour. A little at a time.

A clock change is a future event. Give it a present value.

THE GAUSSIAN MODEL

Spring promises an extra hour. Fall takes one back. DET adds the discounted value of every future change to the Eastern time on your clock.

Faraway changes are worth almost nothing. As a change gets closer, its value approaches exactly +1 or −1 hour. When it arrives, Eastern jumps and that future term disappears. The two movements cancel.

The result is a clock that gently speeds up or slows down, with no daylight-saving jump—even its ticking rate is continuous.

A few things to know+

An experiment, not an official timezone. DET is a proposed clock convention based on Eastern Time. Use regular civil time for appointments. Its date can differ from Eastern near midnight.

A half-value horizon, not a fixed half-life. The Gaussian curve is deliberately flat at the transition, making both the displayed time and its rate continuous. Values use exact elapsed milliseconds, not daily interpolation. The second derivative can change at a transition.

Current rules, projected forward. The calculator supports 2007–2099 and assumes the current US schedule continues: the second Sunday in March and the first Sunday in November. Future rule changes would change DET today. Leap seconds are not modeled.

No early rounding. We add fractional contributions before formatting the clock. The infinite series is truncated only once a conservative bound puts the entire remaining tail below one microsecond. The display follows your device’s clock.
